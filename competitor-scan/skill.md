# Competitor Scan

## 1. Main Purpose

Understand the competitive environment and identify opportunities to differentiate an organisation's communication, positioning and content.

## 2. Input

### 2.1 Competitor Information

- Competitor names from the relevant client knowledge file or context package.
- Existing competitor notes.
- Competitor approval status.
- Previous competitor research, where available.
- Research period or observation requirements.

Treat unapproved competitors as a provisional research shortlist, not as confirmed competitors. Do not assume a fixed number of competitors.

### 2.2 Competitor Record Fields

- Competitor ID
- Company name
- Website
- LinkedIn page
- Headquarters
- Primary markets
- Service portfolio
- Target industries
- Target audience
- Positioning
- Content pillars
- Content formats
- Publishing frequency
- Engagement observations
- Proof points
- Differentiation opportunity
- Evidence URL
- Observation date

### 2.3 Research Sources

Use publicly available sources, including:

- Company websites.
- Public LinkedIn pages.
- Public case studies.
- Public testimonials.
- Public certifications.
- Other relevant public sources.

## 3. Instructions

1. Read the competitor names, existing research notes and approval status from the relevant client knowledge files and current context package.

2. Identify whether each competitor is approved or provisional. Do not present an unapproved competitor as a confirmed competitor.

3. Research competitors using publicly available information only.

4. Examine their positioning, service portfolio, target industries and target audience.

5. Review their LinkedIn communication, including content pillars, formats and publishing frequency.

6. Examine how competitors communicate AI, business outcomes, deliverables, original frameworks and perspectives.

7. Review their case studies, testimonials and certifications for specific and credible proof points.

8. Identify the buyer personas they communicate with.

9. Identify content gaps and opportunities to communicate a distinct point of view.

10. Record evidence URLs and observation dates for research findings.

11. Clearly distinguish verified findings, observations and information that could not be confirmed.

12. Revalidate competitor observations according to the research frequency or review requirements supplied through the relevant knowledge files or context package.

13. Do not copy competitor content, messaging or distinctive creative.

14. Do not make unsupported superiority claims or assume commercial success from public engagement metrics.

15. Flag missing information, inaccessible sources and unapproved competitor selections instead of making assumptions.

## 4. Output

Produce a structured competitor research report.

### 4.1 Competitor Profile

Include the available competitor record fields:

- Competitor ID
- Company name
- Website
- LinkedIn page
- Headquarters
- Primary markets
- Service portfolio
- Target industries
- Target audience
- Positioning
- Content pillars
- Content formats
- Publishing frequency
- Engagement observations
- Proof points
- Differentiation opportunity
- Evidence URL
- Observation date

### 4.2 Competitive Analysis

Provide findings on:

- Competitor positioning: agency, consultant, technology partner or growth partner.
- AI communication: feature-focused or transformation-focused.
- Business outcomes versus deliverables.
- Original frameworks and perspectives.
- Case study and proof-point credibility.
- Buyer personas.
- Content gaps and differentiation opportunities.

### 4.3 Research Status

Include:

- Competitor approval status.
- Confirmed and provisional competitors.
- Verified findings and unverified information.
- Missing information and research limitations.
- Source URLs and observation dates.
- Next review requirement, if supplied.
